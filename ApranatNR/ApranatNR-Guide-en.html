1. Overview

ApranatNR brings three methods to PixInsight. None of them is part of WBPP (WeightedBatchPreprocessing). The methods were published by apranat on his blog (in Japanese); ApranatNR is an independent implementation.

  • Drunk (RTN) pixel correction (software pixel mapping): original article
  • Cool file correction: original article
  • Cosmicut: original article

There are two ways to use it.

  • A. Standalone script (ApranatNR.js): corrects frames calibrated by WBPP; registration and integration follow in WBPP or another tool. Good for checking each step.
  • B. Inside WBPP (ApranatNR-WBPP-Builder.js): uses WBPP's "Pipeline builder" feature to insert the ApranatNR steps into WBPP's own pipeline. One WBPP run does everything.

Processing flow (inside WBPP)

  1. Master dark and master flat WBPP (unchanged)
  2. Calibration of the lights WBPP
  3. ① ApranatNR: drunk pixel correction → cool file correction ApranatNR
  4. Cosmetic correction, debayering WBPP
  5. Registration, integration → master light WBPP
  6. ② Cosmicut master (optional, for comparison) ApranatNR

2. The three methods

Drunk pixel correction①

Pixels whose output jumps between two values from frame to frame (RTN: random telegraph noise) are replaced by the value of their surroundings.

Cool file correction①

Pixels that come out darker than their surroundings (cool pixels) are found and lifted by that amount in every frame.

Cosmicut②

After registration, each pixel is averaged without its brightest frame. Cosmic rays and satellite trails disappear.

2.1 Drunk (RTN) pixel correction (software pixel mapping)

A drunk pixel jumps between two levels from frame to frame because of a defect in its readout circuit. Its position is fixed for each sensor. A master dark only holds each pixel's average, so dark subtraction does not remove the jumps.

Detection: individual dark frames are used (8 or more, preferably about 20). Besides FITS and XISF, camera raw files such as CR2 are read as undebayered sensor values.

  • For each pixel, the fluctuation (standard deviation) over the frames is computed without the pixel's highest and lowest value, so that one-off events such as cosmic rays do not cause false detections.
  • This fluctuation is compared with the fluctuation expected at the pixel's dark level; pixels above 4 × (1.5 to 20 in the settings) are drunk pixels. The expected fluctuation follows the physics of a dark frame:
    expected² = read noise² + slope × (dark level − normal dark level)
    The second term is the shot noise of the dark current. The read noise is the median fluctuation of all pixels; the slope comes from "warm" pixels well above the normal level. Hot pixels that only have much dark current fluctuate more because they are brighter, exactly as the formula says, so they are not detected.
    The dark level used is not the pixel's mean but its lower quartile (mean − 0.674 × fluctuation). A drunk pixel that jumps upward now and then has a raised mean and would look like a warm pixel; its lower quartile stays at the normal level (this type is common in darks of DSLRs such as the Canon EOS 6D).
  • The result is saved as the defect map rtn_defect_map.xisf. Above 3% of all pixels the run stops with an error, since the darks are probably faulty (saturated, for example); a high-gain ASI533MC Pro gives about 1.2%.

Repair: in each calibrated, undebayered light, a defect pixel is replaced by the median of the surrounding pixels of the same color (for color cameras, the 8 pixels two pixels away).

Structure protection (on by default): a pixel is not repaired when the replacement would change it by more than 5σ (the spread among same-color pixels of that frame).

  • A star's center is always brighter than its surroundings, so replacing it dims the star. Without guiding or dithering, the same star stays on the same defect pixel in every frame and the loss remains in the master. The protection prevents this.
  • A faint star buried in the noise of each frame escapes that test. So the unregistered stack of the uncorrected lights (each pixel averaged without its brightest frame, so that cosmic rays do not look like stars) is used as well. A defect pixel is not repaired when either applies:
    • the pixel is brighter than its same-color neighbors by more than 5σ;
    • its 8 adjacent pixels are brighter than their own same-color neighbors, together, by more than 3σ (a star's footprint; the trace of a drunk pixel covers one pixel only).
    On data taken unguided and without dithering (27 × 10 s, the field hardly moves), 1.43% of the defect pixels were left unrepaired (991 by their own excess, 1,142 by a footprint). For the 218 faint stars whose core sits on a defect pixel, the median flux change went from −3.5% (1.5.0) to +0.1%, and stars dimmed by more than 10% went from 31 to 3. The noise reduction and the defect repair were unchanged.
  • Large one-off outliers on defect pixels (cosmic rays) are also left to the pixel rejection of the integration.
  • Defect pixels whose value is NaN or infinite are always replaced.

2.2 Cool file correction

  • The lights are averaged without registration. As long as the field drifts a little, stars and nebulae blur while the dark points of the sensor stay in place.
  • Pixels darker than the median of their same-color neighbors by 5σ or more (0 to 10 in the settings; 0 is the article's "threshold zero") are cool pixels; the difference (the lift) is recorded in the "cool file".
  • The cool file is added to those pixels in every frame. Other pixels do not change.
  • Structure protection (on by default): only isolated dark points on flat surroundings are corrected. Star images and nebula edges are excluded, because shot noise and brightness changes make pixels there look dark.
  • It works with 2 frames or more; 10 or more are recommended.

2.3 Cosmicut

  • For registered frames, each pixel becomes (sum − maximum) ÷ (number of frames − 1): an average that always drops the brightest frame.
  • This is the same calculation as Min/Max rejection in ImageIntegration (1 high, 0 low).
  • Image borders left by the registration (values at or below 0) can be excluded as no data.
  • At least 3 frames are needed.

4. Use A: standalone script

Run ApranatNR.js with "Script > Execute Script File...". After adding its folder in "Script > Feature Scripts...", it starts from "Script > Preprocessing > ApranatNR".

Tab ① Before registration (drunk pixels / cool file)

  1. Add to Light frames the _c.xisf files from WBPP's calibrated folder (dark/flat calibrated, not registered, not debayered).
  2. Leave Sensor pattern on "Automatic". In undebayered color images, only pixels of the same color are used.
  3. For drunk pixel correction, check its group title and give one of:
    • Dark frames: individual darks with the same settings as the lights (8 or more)
    • Existing defect map: an rtn_defect_map.xisf made earlier (the dark analysis is skipped)
    "Fluctuation ratio threshold" (default 4) and "Protect stars and other structures (recommended)" can stay as they are.
  4. The cool file correction "Detection threshold (σ)" (default 5) and its structure protection can stay as they are.
  5. With an empty Output folder, an ApranatNR subfolder is created next to the first light. The default Output file suffix is _anr.
  6. Press "① Run". Integrate the corrected frames in WBPP (register only the lights, without calibration masters) or register them with StarAlignment.

Tab ② After registration (Cosmicut)

  1. Add the Registered frames.
  2. Normally keep "Treat pixels at or below 0 as no data" on.
  3. "② Run Cosmicut" writes master_cosmicut.xisf. With "Also save the map of rejected maximum values", the image of the dropped maxima is saved too.

With "Overwrite existing files" off, files that already exist get new names ending in _1, _2, ... The original lights are never overwritten.

Language: the dialog opens in English. Choose "日本語" under "Language" at the bottom to switch to Japanese; the entries are kept and the choice is remembered. Console messages stay in English.

5. Use B: inside WBPP

  1. In WBPP's "Pipeline" tab, enable "Pipeline builder" and select ApranatNR-WBPP-Builder.js. The pipeline view then shows an "ApranatNR" step right after Calibration for each light group.
  2. Add lights, darks and flats as usual.
    • With 8 or more individual darks matching the lights, the drunk pixel defect map is built automatically on every run.
    • When only a master dark is added, set an existing defect map in rtnDefectMapPath of the settings file. Otherwise the drunk pixel correction is skipped with a warning.
  3. Run. WBPP debayers and processes the frames corrected by ApranatNR.

Errors: a mistake in the settings file is shown in WBPP's pipeline view, and a run stops before the lights are processed. Processing never continues silently without the correction.

  • The corrected frames need disk space for one more set of calibrated frames (included in WBPP's free space check).
  • Typical time: 20 to 30 seconds for 20 to 27 frames of 3008 × 3008 pixels. Results are not cached; each run redoes the work.
  • The master dark and master flat are not changed.

8. Reusing a defect map

An rtn_defect_map.xisf can be used for other sessions when:

  • it comes from the same camera (positions differ between sensors);
  • image size, binning and orientation are the same (the size is checked, a vertical flip is not; darks and lights from the same capture software are fine);
  • gain and temperature are preferably the same (positions do not change, but the size and frequency of the jumps do, so the detected pixels shift a little; a Gain 460 map has helped Gain 400 lights).
  • Drunk pixels come and go, so one set of 24 darks finds about 60% of them. Maps from darks of other days or temperatures share 60–70% of their pixels (darks of 80 ms at Gain 460 and of 10 s at Gain 400 agreed on 74%).
  • Defect pixels increase over the years; rebuild the map whenever you take new darks.

Whether the master dark used for the calibration is new or old does not matter, because the correction works on the calibrated lights.

10. Notes and known limitations

The structure protection is too strict in low-noise frames. It judges by how much the replacement changes a pixel. In darks without sky noise, or in short narrowband exposures under a very dark sky, the drunk pixel jumps themselves exceed 5σ, so background pixels that are not stars are left unrepaired too. In a test on darks, about 27% of the map was not repaired (0.6–1.5% in the RGB lights so far). For such data the protection can be turned off.

  • Master darks are not corrected. A drunk pixel's value in a master dark is its average dark level, which is right to subtract; the pixel is replaced in the lights.
  • The defect map is built from individual darks. A master dark cannot be used, as it has no information on the fluctuation over time.
  • The WBPP integration uses WBPP's official Pipeline builder, but how the step is inserted depends on WBPP's internal structure. After a major WBPP update, check it with the end-to-end test below.
  • WBPP remembers the previous file lists. Darks or flats of another target left in the lists can calibrate the lights with darks of another exposure or gain, and the defect map is then built from those darks. After a run, check IC.masterDarkPath and the ApranatNR line "Dark 1/24: …" in the log.
  • WBPP's automatic reference frame selection can change from run to run. To compare masters, use the same reference frame, or align positions and intensity scales first.
  • The protection of faint stars (the stack test) works for undithered data, where the same star stays on the same pixels. In the unguided, undithered test data, 3 of the 218 faint stars whose core sits on a defect pixel were still dimmed by more than 10%.
  • In darks whose temperature changed during the sequence, the dark current of bright hot pixels changes too, so they fluctuate more than the formula (read noise + shot noise) predicts. A few dozen hot pixels may then be marked as drunk pixels.

11. FAQ

What are the dark frames for?
Only to locate the drunk pixels; they are not subtracted from the lights. Without light, changes over time come from the sensor noise alone, so abnormally fluctuating pixels are easy to tell apart.
Once I have a defect map, do I still need darks?
No. Give it as "Existing defect map" in the standalone script, or as rtnDefectMapPath inside WBPP.
Do I use the lights before debayering?
Yes: calibrated, undebayered single-channel images. For color cameras, pixels of the same color are compared with each other.
Does it help against noise that gets through WBPP's sigma clipping (ESD rejection)?
It helps against the noise that rejection handles poorly. Rejection drops values that stand out in a minority of frames at the same sky position, so these get through: ① defects that land on the same position in every frame without dithering, ② drunk pixels that jump in about half of the frames, ③ defect pixels when there are few frames. The drunk pixel correction knows the defect positions from the darks and repairs them frame by frame. Background noise fell by about 1.7% on data taken unguided and without dithering, and by about 1.2–1.4% on data whose field shifts between frames (like dithering). One-off outliers such as cosmic rays are handled well by ESD rejection, so Cosmicut adds almost nothing there.
Can it make apranat's "median brightness vs brightness fluctuation" plot of darks?
Yes, with the separate tool DarkPixelPlot (DarkPixelPlot/DarkPixelPlot.js). Select individual darks (8 or more, raw files included) and it writes the plot of each pixel's median against its standard deviation as a PNG. An ApranatNR defect map can be overlaid. It marks pixels with the same criterion as ApranatNR (read noise + dark current shot noise, at each pixel's lower quartile) and draws the typical fluctuation with this formula. Its dialog and plot are available in English and Japanese.
Why does the structure protection act on darks? Darks have no stars.
They have none. The protection judges by the size of the change only, so in low-noise darks the drunk pixel jumps themselves exceed the limit (see "Notes and known limitations").

12. Development and tests

After changing the code, regenerate the WBPP builder file. WBPP's builder cannot use #include, so the sources are merged into one generated file.

node tools/build-wbpp-builder.js

There are three kinds of tests (run in the ApranatNR folder).

# unit tests (Node)
node --test tests/*.test.js

# standalone script test (runs in a separate PixInsight instance)
/Applications/PixInsight/PixInsight.app/Contents/MacOS/PixInsight -n --automation-mode \
  --no-startup-scripts --no-startup-check-updates --no-splash \
  -r="$PWD/tests/ApranatNR-SmokeTest.js" --force-exit

# end-to-end test of the WBPP integration (runs the real WBPP on synthetic data, about 40 s)
tests/wbpp-e2e/run.sh

The unit tests also check that the generated builder file matches its sources, and that every dialog message exists in both languages. WBPP's automation mode does not save WBPP's settings.

During the end-to-end test, PixInsight itself occasionally crashes (while WBPP runs or on exit); the later checks then fail although the ApranatNR results passed. Run it again. If PixInsight starts extremely slowly after a crash, quit every PixInsight instance and start it again.
